## Checklist

### Molecular biochemistry

_Scope:_ DNA and RNA structure, nucleotide synthesis and salvage, replication, repair, transcription, RNA processing, translation and protein modification. Step 1 tests the molecular step that fails in a disease or that a drug blocks, not memorized enzyme lists for their own sake.

#### Chromatin and nucleotides

- **Chromatin structure and epigenetic regulation** — ⬜ missing · understand · priority 2 · `bc-chromatin-structure`
  - Outline: Multisystem Processes & Disorders › Genetic, metabolic, and developmental disorders (foundational)
  - Elements: mechanism
  - Must know:
    - Nucleosome: DNA wrapped around an octamer of histones (H2A, H2B, H3, H4) with H1 linking; heterochromatin condensed and inactive, euchromatin open and active.
    - DNA methylation (CpG islands) silences transcription; histone acetylation relaxes chromatin and activates, deacetylation silences.
    - Links to imprinting, X-inactivation and fragile X (methylation of the expanded repeat).
- **De novo purine and pyrimidine synthesis** — 🟡 partial · understand · priority 3 · `bc-nucleotide-synthesis`
  - Outline: Blood & Lymphoreticular System › Anemia, cytopenias, and polycythemia (foundational); Multisystem Processes & Disorders › Adverse effects of drugs on multisystem disorders (foundational)
  - Elements: enzyme-pathway, pharmacology, disease
  - Must know:
    - Purines built on ribose-5-phosphate (PRPP); pyrimidines made as a ring (orotate) then attached to PRPP.
    - Ribonucleotide reductase makes deoxyribonucleotides; thymidylate synthase makes dTMP using folate.
    - Drugs by step: methotrexate and trimethoprim (dihydrofolate reductase), 5-fluorouracil (thymidylate synthase), hydroxyurea (ribonucleotide reductase), leflunomide (dihydroorotate dehydrogenase), mycophenolate (IMP dehydrogenase), 6-mercaptopurine (purine synthesis).
    - Hereditary orotic aciduria (UMP synthase): megaloblastic anemia not corrected by B12 or folate, no hyperammonemia.
  - Existing content: `otc-deficiency`
  - Gap: Only orotic aciduria vs OTC is covered; the pathway and drug targets are absent.
- **Purine salvage and its deficiencies** — 🟡 partial · apply · priority 3 · `bc-purine-salvage`
  - Outline: Immune System › Disorders associated with immunodeficiency; Musculoskeletal System › Degenerative and metabolic disorders
  - Elements: enzyme-pathway, disease, genetics, presentation
  - Must know:
    - HGPRT salvages hypoxanthine and guanine; Lesch–Nyhan syndrome (X-linked HGPRT deficiency): hyperuricemia, gout, intellectual disability, self-mutilation, dystonia.
    - Adenosine deaminase deficiency: toxic dATP accumulation inhibits ribonucleotide reductase → lymphocyte death → SCID (autosomal recessive).
    - Xanthine oxidase converts hypoxanthine → xanthine → uric acid (allopurinol, febuxostat).
  - Existing content: `gout`

### Genetics

_Scope:_ Inheritance patterns, genetic terms that explain atypical pedigrees, population genetics, and the high-yield single-gene and chromosomal disorders. Step 1 tests pedigree interpretation, recurrence risk, the molecular mechanism and the presentation; it does not test management.

#### Genetic principles

- **Penetrance, expressivity, pleiotropy and heterogeneity** — ⬜ missing · apply · priority 3 · `bc-gen-terms`
  - Outline: Multisystem Processes & Disorders › Genetic, metabolic, and developmental disorders (foundational)
  - Elements: genetics
  - Must know:
    - Incomplete penetrance: not everyone with the genotype shows the phenotype (e.g. BRCA1).
    - Variable expressivity: same genotype, different severity (e.g. neurofibromatosis type 1).
    - Pleiotropy: one gene, many unrelated effects (e.g. PKU, Marfan syndrome).
    - Locus heterogeneity (different genes, same phenotype, e.g. albinism) vs allelic heterogeneity (different mutations in one gene, e.g. β-thalassemia).
    - Anticipation, loss of heterozygosity, dominant negative effect, linkage disequilibrium, mosaicism (somatic vs gonadal).
- **Genomic imprinting and uniparental disomy** — ⬜ missing · apply · priority 3 · `bc-imprinting`
  - Outline: Multisystem Processes & Disorders › Genetic, metabolic, and developmental disorders; Nervous System & Special Senses › Congenital disorders (foundational)
  - Elements: genetics, disease, presentation
  - Must know:
    - Imprinting: only one parental allele is expressed; the other is silenced by methylation.
    - Prader–Willi syndrome: loss of the paternal chromosome 15q11–13 region (deletion or maternal uniparental disomy); hyperphagia, obesity, hypotonia, hypogonadism, intellectual disability.
    - Angelman syndrome: loss of maternal UBE3A (deletion or paternal uniparental disomy); seizures, ataxia, inappropriate laughter, severe intellectual disability.
    - Uniparental disomy: both copies from one parent; heterodisomy (meiosis I error) vs isodisomy (meiosis II error or postzygotic duplication).
- **Hardy–Weinberg population genetics** — ⬜ missing · apply · priority 3 · `bc-hardy-weinberg`
  - Outline: Multisystem Processes & Disorders › Genetic, metabolic, and developmental disorders (foundational)
  - Elements: genetics
  - Must know:
    - p + q = 1 and p² + 2pq + q² = 1; carrier frequency 2pq ≈ 2q for rare recessive alleles.
    - For X-linked recessive disease, frequency in males = q, in females = q².
    - Assumptions: large population, random mating, no mutation, no selection, no migration.
    - Calculation from disease prevalence: q = √(prevalence) for an autosomal recessive disease.

  - Elements: genetics, mechanism, presentation, labs, histology

    - Essential amino acids; glucogenic vs ketogenic (only leucine and lysine are purely ketogenic); acidic and basic amino acids.

- **Homocystinuria, cystinuria and maple syrup urine disease** — ⬜ missing · apply · priority 3 · `bc-other-aa-disorders`

#### Lipid metabolism and storage diseases

- **Fatty acid synthesis and β-oxidation disorders** — ⬜ missing · apply · priority 3 · `bc-fatty-acid`
  - Outline: Multisystem Processes & Disorders › Genetic, metabolic, and developmental disorders; Nervous System & Special Senses › Neuromuscular disorders; Pregnancy, Childbirth, & the Puerperium › Newborn (birth to 4 weeks of age)
  - Elements: enzyme-pathway, disease, presentation, labs
  - Must know:
    - Synthesis in cytoplasm: citrate shuttle out of mitochondria, acetyl-CoA carboxylase (biotin, insulin-activated) makes malonyl-CoA, which inhibits carnitine acyltransferase I so newly made fatty acids are not oxidized.
    - β-Oxidation in mitochondria: long-chain fatty acids enter through the carnitine shuttle.
    - Systemic primary carnitine deficiency: weakness, hypotonia, hypoketotic hypoglycemia, cardiomyopathy.
    - Medium-chain acyl-CoA dehydrogenase deficiency: hypoketotic hypoglycemia after fasting, vomiting, lethargy, seizures, coma, liver dysfunction; can cause sudden death in infants; avoid fasting.
- **Ketone bodies** — 🟡 partial · apply · priority 3 · `bc-ketones`
  - Outline: Endocrine System › Diabetes mellitus and other disorders of the endocrine pancreas (foundational); Behavioral Health › Substance use disorders; Multisystem Processes & Disorders › Fluid, electrolyte, and acid-base balance disorders (foundational)
  - Elements: enzyme-pathway, mechanism, labs
  - Must know:
    - Liver makes acetoacetate and β-hydroxybutyrate from fatty-acid-derived acetyl-CoA when oxaloacetate is diverted to gluconeogenesis (prolonged fasting, DKA) or consumed by high NADH (alcohol use).
    - The liver cannot use ketones (lacks succinyl-CoA–acetoacetate CoA transferase); heart, muscle and brain (in starvation) use them; RBCs cannot.
    - Breath smells of acetone; urine nitroprusside test detects acetoacetate, not β-hydroxybutyrate (alcoholic ketoacidosis may give a falsely low result).
  - Existing content: `diabetes-types`, `anion-gap`
  - Gap: Ketoacidosis appears within DKA and anion-gap concepts; the synthesis pathway and the nitroprusside limitation are missing.
- **Fed state, fasting and starvation** — ⬜ missing · apply · priority 2 · `bc-fed-fasting`
  - Outline: Multisystem Processes & Disorders › Nutrition; Endocrine System › Diabetes mellitus and other disorders of the endocrine pancreas (foundational)
  - Elements: mechanism, enzyme-pathway
  - Must know:
    - Fed state (insulin): glycolysis, glycogenesis, fatty acid and protein synthesis, HMP shunt.
    - Fasting (glucagon, epinephrine): hepatic glycogenolysis first (stores last about a day), then gluconeogenesis, lipolysis and ketogenesis.
    - Starvation beyond about 3 days: adipose stores supply fatty acids and ketones become the main brain fuel, sparing protein; survival depends on fat stores; protein breakdown leads to vital organ failure.
    - Insulin vs glucagon effects on key enzymes through phosphorylation (glucagon/cAMP/PKA) and dephosphorylation (insulin).
- **Cholesterol synthesis and steroid precursors** — ⬜ missing · understand · priority 2 · `bc-cholesterol`
  - Outline: Cardiovascular System › Dyslipidemia; Multisystem Processes & Disorders › Adverse effects of drugs on multisystem disorders (foundational)
  - Elements: enzyme-pathway, pharmacology
  - Must know:
    - Acetyl-CoA → HMG-CoA → mevalonate (HMG-CoA reductase, rate limiting; induced by insulin, inhibited by glucagon, cholesterol and statins).
    - Cholesterol is the precursor of bile acids, steroid hormones and vitamin D; lecithin–cholesterol acyltransferase (LCAT) esterifies cholesterol in HDL; cholesteryl ester transfer protein moves esters between lipoproteins.
- **Lipoproteins, apolipoproteins and familial dyslipidemias** — ⬜ missing · apply · priority 3 · `bc-lipoproteins`
  - Outline: Cardiovascular System › Dyslipidemia; Multisystem Processes & Disorders › Genetic, metabolic, and developmental disorders
  - Elements: enzyme-pathway, disease, genetics, labs, presentation
  - Must know:
    - Chylomicrons (dietary triglycerides; apo B-48, C-II, E), VLDL (hepatic triglycerides; apo B-100), IDL, LDL (cholesterol to tissues; apo B-100), HDL (reverse transport; apo A-I activates LCAT). Lipoprotein lipase (apo C-II cofactor) hydrolyzes triglycerides; apo E mediates remnant uptake.
    - Abetalipoproteinemia (MTP deficiency, no apo B-48/B-100): fat malabsorption, steatorrhea, acanthocytes, ataxia, retinitis pigmentosa (fat-soluble vitamin loss).
    - Familial chylomicronemia (type I: lipoprotein lipase or apo C-II deficiency): pancreatitis, eruptive xanthomas, lipemia retinalis, no increased coronary risk.
    - Familial hypercholesterolemia (type II: LDL receptor, apo B-100 or PCSK9 gain of function; autosomal dominant): very high LDL, tendon (Achilles) xanthomas, corneal arcus, early MI.
    - Dysbetalipoproteinemia (type III, apo E2): palmar and tuberoeruptive xanthomas; hypertriglyceridemia (type IV, hepatic VLDL overproduction): pancreatitis risk.
- **Lysosomal storage diseases** — 🟡 partial · apply · priority 3 · `bc-lysosomal-storage`
  - Outline: Multisystem Processes & Disorders › Genetic, metabolic, and developmental disorders; Nervous System & Special Senses › Congenital disorders (foundational); Musculoskeletal System › Congenital disorders
  - Elements: enzyme-pathway, disease, presentation, histology, genetics
  - Must know:
    - Sphingolipidoses: Tay–Sachs (hexosaminidase A; cherry-red macula, no hepatosplenomegaly, onion-skin lysosomes), Niemann–Pick (sphingomyelinase; cherry-red macula with hepatosplenomegaly, foam cells), Gaucher (glucocerebrosidase; Gaucher cells with crumpled tissue-paper cytoplasm, bone crises, hepatosplenomegaly), Fabry (α-galactosidase A, X-linked; angiokeratomas, painful neuropathy, renal and cardiac disease), Krabbe (galactocerebrosidase; globoid cells, optic atrophy), metachromatic leukodystrophy (arylsulfatase A; central and peripheral demyelination).
    - Mucopolysaccharidoses: Hurler (α-L-iduronidase; gargoylism, corneal clouding, airway obstruction) and Hunter (iduronate-2-sulfatase, X-linked; milder, no corneal clouding, aggressive behavior).
    - All are autosomal recessive except Fabry and Hunter; Ashkenazi Jewish predisposition for Tay–Sachs, Niemann–Pick and Gaucher.
  - Existing content: `gaucher`, `tay-sachs`, `fabry`
  - Gap: Gaucher, Tay–Sachs and Fabry are covered and grouped (grp:lysosomal); Niemann–Pick, Krabbe, metachromatic leukodystrophy, Hurler and Hunter are missing.
